### PART 1 — THE PHYSICAL BASE: MARCHING ON BREAD AND WATER (8–20)
8. "The Gallic Wars. For nine years Caesar's legions fought tribe after tribe across what is now France, Belgium and the Rhine, outnumbered in almost every battle."
9. "His greatest weapon was not the sword. It was speed. Caesar moved whole legions faster than his enemies believed possible."
10. "Roman soldiers marched thirty to forty kilometers a day, carrying more than twenty kilos of armor, weapons, tools and rations on their backs."
11. "They did it on brutally simple fuel. Hard wheat bread, olive oil, sour wine and water. No feasts, no comfort, day after day."
12. "And at the front of that column you would often find Caesar himself, not carried in a litter like other Roman nobles."
13. "He rode ahead of his men, and when the ground turned to swamp he dismounted and marched on foot through the mud beside them."
14. "Bareheaded in the rain and the sun, sleeping in the same camps, eating the same bread. The general suffered everything his soldiers suffered."
15. "His enemies learned to fear it. Tribes would revolt expecting weeks to prepare, and find Caesar's eagles on the horizon within days."

18. "The line held, then advanced. Speed, conditioning and presence. That was the entire system, and it never stopped working."
19. "Here is the lesson. Extreme conditioning plus shared hardship builds a culture that does not break."
20. "When the leader carries the same load, nobody feels entitled to quit. Lead from the front, and respect follows automatically."

21. "Now for the part of Caesar's story that the history books only whisper. His own body was his first enemy."
22. "Plutarch and Suetonius both record it. Caesar suffered from the falling sickness, sudden seizures that could drop him in the middle of a campaign."
23. "Modern historians suspect epilepsy or savage migraines, made worse by everything his life demanded. Chronic stress, and almost no sleep."
24. "In Rome, an illness like that could end a career overnight. Weakness was political death, and his rivals were watching."
25. "Caesar's response was the opposite of hiding. He simply refused to behave like a sick man."
26. "His doctors prescribed rest. He prescribed himself more movement, more campaigns, more miles."
27. "Plutarch writes that he used war itself as his medicine. Long marches, simple food and life outdoors to fight back against his condition."
28. "He treated every symptom as a problem to be out-trained and out-worked, never as an identity to accept."
29. "He made himself a ferocious horseman, famous for riding at full gallop with both hands clasped behind his back."
30. "Years later in Alexandria he went overboard in a sea fight, and swam to safety holding his documents above the water with one hand."
31. "This is the stoic mindset in action, two thousand years before anyone gave it that name."
32. "You do not get to choose your body's weaknesses. You only get to choose your response to them."
33. "Caesar accepted the setback, then used relentless action to build mental armor around it."
34. "The lesson. Action is therapy. When adversity hits, movement beats self-pity, every single time."
