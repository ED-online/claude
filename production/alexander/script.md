# Alexander the Great — Episode 2 (12-min animated documentary)
Format: identical pipeline to Genghis Khan episode. 73 blocks total (welcome + 72 story
blocks), each 10s: seedance_2_0 fast, 16:9, 720p, style key c773f44c reused, Arthur
narration (preset 30fc8796), assembled with explainer_video at 1280x720.

Lessons applied from Genghis v3/v4: cinematic aerial shots (not flat maps) for the big
establishing beats, each locked with a nano-banana end-frame generated first; explicit
geography in every map prompt (never let the model default to a Europe-shaped landmass);
"no text/letters/numbers" in every image and video prompt.

Aerial end-frame beats (8): blocks 11, 21, 24, 33, 41, 48, 53, 70.

## WELCOME (position 1)
NARRATION: Welcome back to the channel. Last time, Genghis Khan. Today — the man every
conqueror since has measured himself against. This is the story of Alexander the Great.
VISUAL: Channel-style parchment globe slowly turning from the Mongolian steppe westward
to the Aegean, warm lamplight, settling on Greece glowing gold.

## ACT 1 — THE BOY (blocks 1–10)

Block 1 (hook)
NARRATION: In eleven years, one man conquered everything from Greece to the edge of
India. He never lost a battle. And he was dead at thirty-two.
VISUAL: Dark parchment world map; a gold stain floods from Macedonia to India in
seconds, then freezes and pulses once.

Block 2 (promise)
NARRATION: This is how a twenty-year-old king destroyed the greatest empire on Earth —
and why the victory destroyed him too.
VISUAL: Camera dives from the map into a torch-lit Macedonian palace courtyard at Pella.

Block 3
NARRATION: Macedonia, three fifty-six BC. To the Greeks in the south it was a
half-barbarian kingdom of shepherds and mountain warlords. They were about to be
corrected.
VISUAL: Aerial of rugged Macedonian highlands, sheep flocks, a small palace town in a
green valley.

Block 4
NARRATION: Its king, Philip the Second, had rebuilt his army into something new: the
phalanx — a wall of eighteen-foot spears no infantry on Earth could break.
VISUAL: Overhead of a phalanx square, sarissa points bristling in perfect rows,
drilling on a plain.

Block 5
NARRATION: His son announced himself early. At twelve, Alexander tamed Bucephalus, a
warhorse no grown man could ride — by turning it away from its own shadow.
VISUAL: Illustration: a boy gripping the mane of a rearing black stallion, its long
shadow stretched behind, watching men frozen mid-laugh.

Block 6
NARRATION: Philip hired the boy a tutor: Aristotle. For three years the future
conqueror of the world studied philosophy, medicine and Homer under its greatest mind.
VISUAL: Illustration: colonnade at Mieza, teacher and pupil over scrolls, an army
drilling tiny in the valley below.

Block 7
NARRATION: At eighteen, Alexander led Philip's cavalry at Chaeronea and shattered the
armies of Athens and Thebes. Greece now belonged to Macedon.
VISUAL: Map battle: two blue city-state blocks buckle as a gold cavalry wedge crashes
through their line.

Block 8
NARRATION: Philip's next war was already planned: the invasion of Persia — the
superpower that had burned Athens a century before. He would never fight it.
VISUAL: Map zoom-out: tiny gold Macedon against the vast purple Persian Empire
stretching from the Aegean to India.

Block 9
NARRATION: In three thirty-six BC, at his own daughter's wedding, Philip was cut down
by his bodyguard. His heir was twenty years old.
VISUAL: Illustration: torch-lit wedding procession in a theatre entrance, a golden
crown rolling on stone steps. No violence shown.

Block 10
NARRATION: Rivals expected the boy-king to fall. Instead, when Thebes rebelled,
Alexander erased the city and sold its people. Greece went silent.
VISUAL: Map: the Thebes marker cracks apart and fades to ash-grey; surrounding city
icons dim submissively.

## ACT 2 — PERSIA INVADED (blocks 11–26)

Block 11 — AERIAL END-FRAME
NARRATION: In three thirty-four he crossed into Asia with forty thousand men — and
hurled a spear into the beach, claiming the whole continent as his prize.
VISUAL: Aerial of the Hellespont strait: a swarm of tiny ships mid-channel, the Asian
shore golden in morning light. End-frame locked.

Block 12
NARRATION: Persia's local governors met him at the river Granicus. Their plan was
simple: kill the young king on the first day, and end the war instantly.
VISUAL: Map: a river line, purple cavalry blocks massed on the far bank, a small gold
arrow approaching.

Block 13
NARRATION: Alexander charged the river himself, in white-plumed armour anyone could
see. An axe split his helmet — but the Persian line broke.
VISUAL: Illustration: Companion cavalry surging through river spray up a muddy bank,
white plume at the front.

Block 14
NARRATION: Instead of chasing the Persians inland, he marched down the coast, taking
every port — strangling Persia's great navy without fighting a single sea battle.
VISUAL: Map: gold tide rolling down the Ionian coastline as anchor icons wink out one
by one.

Block 15
NARRATION: At Gordium stood a wagon tied with an impossible knot. Whoever untied it,
legend said, would rule Asia. Alexander drew his sword and cut it.
VISUAL: Illustration: sword flashing down through an ancient gnarled knot on a wagon
yoke, onlookers recoiling.

Block 16
NARRATION: Then came news: Darius, Great King of Persia, was marching to meet him in
person — with an army perhaps five times his size.
VISUAL: Map: an enormous purple mass creeping west; the gold arrow looks suddenly
very small.

Block 17
NARRATION: At Issus the armies met on a narrow strip between mountains and sea —
ground where Persia's numbers meant nothing. Alexander charged straight at Darius.
VISUAL: Map battle: tight corridor between mountain hatching and coast; gold wedge
punches toward a purple royal star.

Block 18
NARRATION: The Great King turned his chariot and fled — abandoning his army, and his
own mother, wife and daughters, who fell into Alexander's hands.
VISUAL: Illustration: an empty royal chariot, a dropped cloak and bow, dust hanging
over an abandoned camp.

Block 19
NARRATION: Everyone expected the captives to be enslaved. Instead he treated Darius's
family as royalty. He wasn't just conquering Persia. He was auditioning to rule it.
VISUAL: Illustration: royal tent interior, Alexander gesturing courteously to bowing
Persian noblewomen.

Block 20
NARRATION: Darius offered half his empire for peace. Old general Parmenion urged: "I
would accept, if I were Alexander." The reply: "So would I — if I were Parmenion."
VISUAL: Illustration: a letter and chests of gold on a campaign table, Alexander
smiling faintly, Parmenion frowning.

Block 21 — AERIAL END-FRAME
NARRATION: One city defied him: Tyre, an island fortress half a mile offshore, walls
rising sheer from the sea. It had never been taken.
VISUAL: Aerial of an island city ringed by high sea-walls, waves breaking at their
base, mainland shore distant. End-frame locked.

Block 22
NARRATION: So Alexander did the impossible: he built a bridge. For seven months his
engineers dumped stone into the sea, pushing a causeway toward the walls.
VISUAL: Aerial/map hybrid: a pale causeway creeping across blue water toward the
island, siege towers inching along it.

Block 23
NARRATION: When Tyre finally fell, the price of defiance was displayed to the world.
No city on that coast resisted him again.
VISUAL: Map: down the Levantine coast, city icons flip to gold in rapid sequence.

Block 24 — AERIAL END-FRAME
NARRATION: Egypt didn't even fight. After two centuries of hated Persian rule it
welcomed Alexander as a liberator — and crowned him Pharaoh.
VISUAL: Aerial of the green Nile threading golden desert, temples and a procession of
boats on the river. End-frame locked.

Block 25
NARRATION: On the coast he marked out a new city in barley flour: Alexandria. It would
outlive his empire by two thousand years.
VISUAL: Map-plan: glowing grid lines of a city being drawn on an empty shoreline
between sea and lake.

Block 26
NARRATION: Then he vanished into the desert, to the oracle at Siwa. What was said
there stayed secret — but Alexander returned calling himself the son of Zeus.
VISUAL: Aerial: a single green oasis ringed by an ocean of dunes, one tiny caravan
track leading in.

## ACT 3 — KING OF ASIA (blocks 27–45)

Block 27
NARRATION: In three thirty-one BC he turned inland at last, crossing the Euphrates and
the Tigris, hunting Darius — who had spent two years building an army to end him.
VISUAL: Map: gold arrow crossing two great rivers eastward into Mesopotamia; purple
mass waiting beyond.

Block 28
NARRATION: At Gaugamela, Darius chose the battlefield himself: a vast plain, flattened
for his chariots — room for a quarter of a million men to swallow forty-seven thousand.
VISUAL: Map: an immense purple crescent dwarfing a compact gold block on an open plain.

Block 29
NARRATION: His generals begged him to attack at night. Alexander refused — "I will not
steal my victory" — and slept so soundly they had to wake him for the battle.
VISUAL: Illustration: the king asleep in a plain tent at dawn, anxious officers at the
flap, battle lines visible beyond.

Block 30
NARRATION: Alexander rode wide to the right, dragging the Persian line with him,
stretching it — until a gap tore open at its centre. Exactly where he wanted it.
VISUAL: Map battle: gold cavalry sliding right, purple line stretching, a dark gap
opening at the middle.

Block 31
NARRATION: Into that gap he drove his Companion cavalry in a single wedge — aimed,
again, directly at Darius. And again, the Great King ran.
VISUAL: Map battle: gold wedge lancing through the gap at the royal star, which flees
off the map's eastern edge.

Block 32
NARRATION: Persia's last great army dissolved. The empire that had ruled the world for
two centuries now belonged, in all but name, to a twenty-five-year-old.
VISUAL: Map: purple drains to gold across all Mesopotamia in one slow wave.

Block 33 — AERIAL END-FRAME
NARRATION: Babylon opened its gates without a fight. Alexander rode beneath the Ishtar
Gate through streets carpeted with flowers.
VISUAL: Aerial of Babylon: blue-tiled gate, ziggurat, palm-lined river through the
city, crowds like coloured grains. End-frame locked.

Block 34
NARRATION: At Persepolis, the ceremonial heart of Persia, discipline finally broke.
After a night of drinking, the palace of the Great Kings burned to the ground.
VISUAL: Illustration: great columned terraces silhouetted against a spreading orange
glow, sparks in the night sky. No people harmed shown.

Block 35
NARRATION: He chased Darius for weeks — and found him dying in a wagon, stabbed by his
own nobles. Alexander covered the Great King with his own cloak.
VISUAL: Illustration: a lone wagon in wilderness at dusk, a red cloak being laid
gently over a still figure.

Block 36
NARRATION: The war of revenge was over. But Alexander didn't turn home. He now called
himself King of Asia — and Asia was bigger than any Greek had imagined.
VISUAL: Map zoom-out eastward: beyond Persia the parchment fades to blank, unmapped
space.

Block 37
NARRATION: Bessus, the satrap who had murdered Darius, crowned himself Great King.
Alexander pursued him into Afghanistan — through country armies simply did not cross.
VISUAL: Aerial: the Hindu Kush, a wall of snow peaks; one thread-thin column entering
a shadowed valley.

Block 38
NARRATION: He took the army over the Hindu Kush in spring snow — seventeen days on
frozen passes, eating the pack animals. Bessus never believed he would come.
VISUAL: Aerial: a winding line of soldiers on a white pass between crags, wind-blown
snow streaming off ridges.

Block 39
NARRATION: Bessus's own men handed him over in chains. But the execution solved
nothing — behind Alexander, Sogdiana rose in full revolt.
VISUAL: Map: across Sogdiana, red rebellion icons flare up one after another.

Block 40
NARRATION: For two years the invincible army fought shadows: horse-archers who
scattered when charged, fortresses on cliffs, ambushed garrisons. His hardest war.
VISUAL: Map: red sparks igniting and vanishing across the region while gold columns
chase and miss.

Block 41 — AERIAL END-FRAME
NARRATION: One stronghold crowned sheer cliffs. Its defenders told Alexander he would
need soldiers with wings. Three hundred climbers took it in a single night.
VISUAL: Aerial at dawn: an impossible flat-topped rock fortress above cliffs, rope
lines glinting on the north face. End-frame locked.

Block 42
NARRATION: Among the captives was Roxana, daughter of a Bactrian lord — by every
account one of the most beautiful women in Asia. Alexander married her.
VISUAL: Illustration: wedding scene, bread cut with a sword shared between bride and
groom, two courts watching.

Block 43
NARRATION: But the king was changing. At a drunken banquet his officer Cleitus — who
had once saved his life — mocked him. In his rage, Alexander killed him on the spot.
VISUAL: Illustration: an overturned banquet table, wine spreading like a shadow,
horrified faces, the king frozen. No gore.

Block 44
NARRATION: He wore Persian dress now, and demanded Persian court ritual. To his weary
Macedonians, their king was becoming the very thing they had marched to destroy.
VISUAL: Illustration: mirror-split figure — half Macedonian armour, half Persian robes
and diadem — courtiers divided on each side.

Block 45
NARRATION: Conspiracies followed, even among his own royal pages. Alexander executed
the plotters — and with them his official historian. Aristotle's pupil was gone.
VISUAL: Illustration: a scroll curling to ash on a brazier in a dark tent.

## ACT 4 — INDIA AND THE END (blocks 46–66)

Block 46
NARRATION: And still he went on. In three twenty-seven he crossed into India, chasing
the edge of the world — which Greek maps placed just beyond the next river.
VISUAL: Map: gold arrow crossing into a parchment edge where the drawn world
dissolves into blank speculation.

Block 47
NARRATION: Some Indian kings allied with him. But across the river Hydaspes waited
King Porus — with an army built around two hundred war elephants.
VISUAL: Map: a broad river line; beyond it, ranks of elephant icons and a tall royal
marker.

Block 48 — AERIAL END-FRAME
NARRATION: The monsoon had broken. The Hydaspes ran a mile wide in flood. Crossing it
under Porus's eyes was impossible. Alexander crossed it anyway.
VISUAL: Aerial in rain: a swollen brown-silver river coiling through green plains
under towering monsoon clouds. End-frame locked.

Block 49
NARRATION: Night after night he marched troops noisily along the bank, until Porus
stopped reacting. Then, in a thunderstorm, he slipped upstream and crossed at dawn.
VISUAL: Map: repeated feint arrows along the bank, then one true arrow hooking
upstream through storm-darkened parchment.

Block 50
NARRATION: The elephants charged — and shattered against ranks that opened lanes and
closed again. His masterpiece, and the hardest-fought battle his veterans had known.
VISUAL: Map battle: elephant icons churning inside a flexing gold lattice that bends
but never breaks.

Block 51
NARRATION: Wounded and beaten, Porus was brought before him. Asked how he wished to be
treated, he said: "Like a king." Alexander gave him back his kingdom — enlarged.
VISUAL: Illustration: a tall wounded king standing unbowed before Alexander, who
rises to meet him.

Block 52
NARRATION: By the river he founded a city and named it not for himself but for his
horse. Bucephalus had carried him from Macedonia to India — and died there.
VISUAL: Illustration: a black horse silhouette on a riverbank at sunset, a new city's
scaffolding rising behind.

Block 53 — AERIAL END-FRAME
NARRATION: Then, at the river Hyphasis, the army that had marched twenty thousand
kilometres simply stopped. They wept. They begged. And they refused to go further.
VISUAL: Aerial: a rain-soaked camp of thousands, ranks standing motionless, facing an
endless green horizon east. End-frame locked.

Block 54
NARRATION: Alexander sulked in his tent for three days, like Achilles. The army held.
For the first time in his life he was defeated — by his own men.
VISUAL: Illustration: a sealed royal tent in the rain, silent soldiers waiting in the
mud around it.

Block 55
NARRATION: He built twelve giant altars at the river's edge — monuments to the gods at
the limit of his world — and turned back.
VISUAL: Aerial: twelve colossal stone altars in a line along the riverbank, tiny
figures at their bases.

Block 56
NARRATION: The road back nearly killed him anyway. Storming a Mallian town, he leapt
from the wall alone among the enemy — and an arrow punched into his lung.
VISUAL: Illustration: one figure atop a wall against a white sky, ladder breaking
below him. No wound shown.

Block 57
NARRATION: His men, certain he was dead, went berserk. When Alexander appeared alive
days later, the army wept twice — once in grief, once in relief.
VISUAL: Illustration: the pale king carried on a shield past roaring, reaching ranks
of soldiers.

Block 58
NARRATION: The route home led through the Gedrosian desert. Sixty days of heat and
thirst killed more of his soldiers than every battle of the campaign combined.
VISUAL: Aerial: an ocean of dunes in white heat, a column dissolving into shimmer,
gear abandoned along the track.

Block 59
NARRATION: Scouts found one helmet of water and offered it to the king. In front of
the army, Alexander poured it into the sand. He would thirst with them.
VISUAL: Illustration: a helmet tipped over, a bright ribbon of water vanishing into
sand, ringed by cracked lips and silence.

Block 60
NARRATION: Back in Persia he found his empire rotting — governors who had bet on his
death had plundered their provinces. The reckoning was swift.
VISUAL: Map: corrupt satrap icons across the empire snuffed out one by one like
candles.

Block 61
NARRATION: At Susa he staged a mass wedding: ten thousand of his soldiers married
Persian brides. He dreamed of a new ruling people — half Greek, half Persian.
VISUAL: Illustration: endless wedding tables under awnings, Macedonian and Persian
dress mingling into one crowd.

Block 62
NARRATION: Then Hephaestion died — his closest friend since boyhood, perhaps the only
person he loved without calculation. Alexander broke.
VISUAL: Illustration: the king alone beside a bier, architects' drawings of an
impossibly vast funeral pyre scattered at his feet.

Block 63
NARRATION: In three twenty-three he entered Babylon planning new conquests — Arabia
next, then Carthage, then the western sea. The fleet was already being built.
VISUAL: Map: dotted future arrows curling toward Arabia and far west; shipyard icons
multiplying on the river.

Block 64
NARRATION: In June, after a banquet, came fever. For ten days it climbed, while the
army filed past his bed one by one, in silence. He could no longer speak.
VISUAL: Illustration: an endless line of soldiers moving through a torch-lit palace
hall, each turning his head to the same bed.

Block 65
NARRATION: Alexander the Great died on the tenth of June, three twenty-three BC — one
month short of thirty-three. He had reigned twelve years and eight months.
VISUAL: Map: the whole gold empire holds still — then its light slowly dims to
amber, like an ember.

Block 66
NARRATION: Asked with his last breath who should inherit the empire, he whispered —
so the story goes — "To the strongest."
VISUAL: Illustration: a signet ring passing from an open hand into shadowed, reaching
hands. Faces unseen.

## ACT 5 — THE LONGEST SHADOW (blocks 67–72)

Block 67
NARRATION: The strongest turned out to be everyone. His generals tore the empire apart
in forty years of war. His wife, his son, his mother — all murdered.
VISUAL: Map: the gold empire fractures along jagged lines into rival colours that
grind against each other.

Block 68
NARRATION: Yet the fragments were superpowers in their own right: Ptolemy's Egypt,
Seleucid Asia, Macedonian Greece — Greek-ruled kingdoms from the Nile to Afghanistan.
VISUAL: Map: three great successor kingdoms settle and glow in distinct rich colours.

Block 69
NARRATION: For three centuries, Greek was the language of power from the Mediterranean
to India. That world — the Hellenistic world — was his true empire.
VISUAL: Map: columned-city icons light up in a chain across the whole map, Nile to
Indus. No letters or text.

Block 70 — AERIAL END-FRAME
NARRATION: And Alexandria became the greatest city on Earth — its library holding the
knowledge of the ancient world, its lighthouse counted among its wonders.
VISUAL: Aerial of ancient Alexandria at dusk: the Pharos lighthouse beam sweeping over
harbour, palaces and the library district. End-frame locked.

Block 71
NARRATION: He fought for two decades, against every kind of enemy on Earth, and never
lost a battle. No commander in history has matched that record at that scale.
VISUAL: Map montage: battle stars igniting in sequence — Granicus, Issus, Gaugamela,
Hydaspes — leaving a burning trail.

Block 72 (outro)
NARRATION: Genghis Khan built the largest empire. Alexander cast the longest shadow.
Two thousand years on, conquerors still measure themselves against a man who died at
thirty-two.
VISUAL: Split parchment: the Mongol empire map beside Alexander's, both fading into
one candle-lit map table. Channel end-card mood.
